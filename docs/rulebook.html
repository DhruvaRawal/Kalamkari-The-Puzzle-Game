1The goal

Every player is secretly given one hand-painted Kalamkari design, cut into a 16-piece jigsaw. But the pieces are shuffled together, so your hand holds pieces of everyone's designs, and some of yours are in other players' hands or with the Auction House.

Buy pieces, sell the ones you don't need, and bid for pieces at auction. Be the first to fix all 16 pieces of your design into your board and you win.

2What's in the game

Designs

Four designs: Gaja (Elephant), Qila (Castle), Kamal (Lotus) and Parvat (Mountain). Only the players' designs are used, one per player.

Each is cut into the same 4×4 jigsaw: 4 corners, 8 edges and 4 centres.

Coins

Each player starts with 10 coins. The Auction House starts with 10 coins of its own.

Coins are never created or destroyed. They only move between players and the Auction House.

The Auction House

The bank. It holds the leftover pieces (the pool) and its own coins. It sells you random pieces, buys pieces back from you, and runs the auctions.

Your board

An empty 4×4 frame for your design. A faint outline of your picture shows through the empty slots to help you find where pieces go.

3Setting up

The game does all of this for you:

  1. Each player is secretly given a different design. Only you know which one is yours.
  2. All the pieces of the designs in play are shuffled together.
  3. Each player is dealt 10 random pieces and 10 coins. Your hand can hold pieces of any design, including other players'.
  4. The remaining pieces go to the Auction House as the pool.
  5. A random player goes first. Play then goes clockwise (down the player list).

4Your turn

On your turn you may take each of these two actions once, in either order:

Buy 1 coin

Pay 1 coin to the Auction House and draw a random piece from the pool. Nobody else sees what you drew.

Sell 1 or 2 coins

Choose one of your unfixed pieces. The Auction House pays you straight away, then puts the piece up for auction face-down.

When you're done, end your turn. If you used both Buy and Sell, your turn ends by itself. If you end your turn without doing either, you pass.

How a turn flows

  1. StartYour turn begins and your clock starts.
  2. Buy and/or SellOnce each, in either order. Selling opens an auction.
  3. AuctionYour clock pauses. Other players bid for the piece you sold.
  4. Back to youIf you haven't bought yet, the turn comes back so you can.
  5. End turnPlay passes to the next player.

What the Auction House pays

The price depends on what kind of piece it is in its own design:

  • Corner (4 per design): pays 1 coin
  • Edge (8 per design): pays 2 coins
  • Centre (4 per design): pays 2 coins

The Auction House must be able to pay. If it doesn't have enough coins for the sale, you can't sell that piece. The game tells you why.

5Auctions

Every piece you sell goes straight to auction.

  • Everyone sees that a piece is for sale, whether it's a corner, edge or centre, and what the seller was paid. Nobody sees which piece it is: it's sold face-down.
  • Every player except the seller may bid.
  • Bidding is open: everyone sees each bid. The first bid is at least 1 coin, and each bid must beat the last by at least 1 coin.
  • You can't bid more coins than you have.
  • The auction lasts 15 seconds. After any bid, at least 5 seconds remain, so others always get a chance to answer.
  • There are no ties. An equal bid isn't allowed, so the earlier bid stands.

When the clock runs out

  • Someone bid: the highest bidder pays their bid to the Auction House and takes the piece into their hand.
  • Nobody bid: the piece goes into the pool, where anyone may buy it later.

The turn then goes back to the seller if they haven't bought yet this turn. If they already bought, the turn passes to the next player.

Tip. The seller gets paid by the Auction House, not by the winning bidder. So selling always earns you the fixed price, however much (or little) the piece goes for.

6Placing pieces

You can place pieces at any time: on your turn, on other players' turns, and during auctions. You don't use up an action to place a piece.

  • Drag a piece from your hand onto your board. Rotate it first if it's the wrong way round.
  • A piece locks in only if it's from your design, in its correct slot, and the right way up.
  • If any of those is wrong, the piece goes back to your hand and the game tells you what was wrong: wrong design, wrong slot, or needs rotating. There's no penalty for trying.
  • Pieces from other players' designs never lock into your board. They're only useful for selling.
  • Once a piece is locked it is fixed: you can't move it or sell it.

A ★ marks the pieces in your hand that belong to your design, so you know which ones to keep and which you can sell.

8Winning

Complete

The game ends the moment someone fixes the last of their 16 pieces. They win.

If two players complete at exactly the same moment, the one with more coins wins.

Stalemate

If the pool is empty and every player passes in a row, the game ends. The player with the most fixed pieces wins. Players who tie share the win.

Any Buy or Sell restarts the count.

The end screen shows everyone's coins and fixed count, but never anyone's design.

9Timers

  • Turn clock: each turn lasts 90 seconds by default (60 s, 2 min or 5 min can be chosen instead). When it runs out, your turn ends automatically. If you hadn't bought or sold, that counts as a pass.
  • The ring around the active player's picture is their clock. It drains as time runs out, turns orange in the last third, and red in the last 10 seconds.
  • Selling pauses your clock. While your piece is at auction your clock stops. When the turn comes back to you, it carries on from where it stopped. It doesn't refill.
  • Auction clock: 15 seconds, with at least 5 seconds left after any bid.
  • Disconnecting (online): if you lose your connection on your turn, your clock drops to 15 seconds so the table isn't held up. Reconnect in time and your original clock comes back.

10Pass & play and online

Pass & play

One device, shared around the table.

  • Between turns a privacy curtain covers the screen and asks the next player to take the device. Everyone else looks away.
  • Only the player holding the device can see and arrange their pieces.
  • During an auction, each eligible player has their own row of bid buttons. Bids are public, so there's no curtain for bidding.

Online

Each player on their own device.

  • One player creates a room and shares its 4-letter code. Others join with the code.
  • Everyone can arrange and place their own pieces at any time, even on other players' turns.
  • If you reload the page or drop out, you rejoin your seat automatically.

13Questions and special cases

I have no coins. What can I do?

You can't buy or bid, but you can still sell a piece (which earns you coins) or pass.

The pool is empty.

Buy is disabled. You can still sell. If every player passes in a row while the pool is empty, the game ends in a stalemate.

All my pieces are fixed, but I haven't won yet.

Fixed pieces can't be sold, so Sell is disabled. Buy pieces or win them at auction to finish your design.

The Auction House can't pay for my piece.

Then you can't sell it. Corners cost the Auction House less (1 coin), so you may still be able to sell a corner. Coins flow back to it whenever someone buys or wins an auction.

Can I buy back the piece I just sold?

Not at its auction: the seller can't bid. If nobody bids, it goes into the pool, and you might draw it again later by chance.

Why would I sell a piece of my own design?

You usually wouldn't. The ★ marks your design's pieces in your hand. Sell pieces of other designs instead: they're worth coins to you, and someone else may need them.

Can I place pieces while waiting for my turn?

Yes, online. In pass & play, only the player holding the device can arrange their pieces, so place yours on your own turn.

Quick reference

On your turn (each once)

  • Buy: pay 1 coin, draw a random piece.
  • Sell: get 1 (corner) or 2 (edge, centre) coins; the piece goes to auction.
  • Neither? That's a pass.

Auctions

  • Face-down. The seller can't bid.
  • Bids start at 1, beat the last by 1.
  • 15 s, at least 5 s after each bid.
  • Winner pays the Auction House. No bids: piece to the pool.

Any time

  • Rotate, rearrange and place your pieces.
  • A piece locks only in its own slot of your design, the right way up.
  • Fixed pieces can't move or be sold.

Winning

  • First to fix all 16 pieces wins.
  • Empty pool + everyone passes in a row: most fixed pieces wins.
